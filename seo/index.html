The honest version

SEO has a bad name because a lot of it is sold dishonestly. It is worth saying plainly what I do and what I will not do, before you spend anything.

What the work is

Making the site readable by a search engine, writing pages that answer a real search, and then measuring whether it worked. That is most of it. It is not secret and it is not clever — it is just rarely done properly.

I will not guarantee a position

Nobody outside Google controls the ranking. A guarantee usually means ranking you for a phrase so specific that nobody searches it — which looks like success in a monthly report and produces no work.

Ask anyone promising page one which phrase, and how many people search it.

I will not buy links

Paid link networks work until the algorithm update that discounts them, and then you are worse off than when you started, with an invoice to show for it.

The links worth having come from being worth mentioning.

I will not write for the machine

Pages stuffed with the same phrase read badly to people and, for well over a decade now, badly to Google as well. Forty near-identical town pages are the same mistake at scale.

If a page would embarrass you to read aloud, it is not going to rank.

The work, in four parts

Done roughly in this order, because there is no point writing pages for a site that cannot be crawled.

Can it be crawled, and is it indexed?

Pages blocked by a stray rule, a sitemap listing URLs that redirect, five versions of the same page competing with each other. This is the least interesting part of the job and the one most likely to be quietly costing you everything.

Does it load fast enough?

Speed is a ranking factor, but it matters far more as the reason people leave before the page appears. The fixes are usually images, fonts and scripts nobody chose.

Does it work on a phone?

Google indexes the mobile version. If your site is an afterthought on a phone, that afterthought is what gets ranked.

Structured data, where it earns its place

Marking up your services, questions and details so they can appear as more than a blue link. Useful, quick, and not a substitute for the page being good.

How the site itself gets built

One page, one search

A page trying to rank for eight things ranks for none. The fix is boring and effective: work out the handful of searches that actually bring work, and give each one a page that answers it completely.

The question behind the search

Someone typing a price question wants a price, not a contact form. Pages that answer the actual question outrank pages that dodge it, and they convert better when they do.

Headings that describe the page

One main heading, sub-headings that say what each part covers. It reads better, it is navigable by keyboard and screen reader, and it happens to be what a crawler uses.

Internal links that mean something

Pages linked from nowhere are found by nobody. Linking your related pages to each other, in the words people search, is free and most sites do not bother.

Mentions you can actually get

Suppliers, trade bodies, the local paper, the supplier whose product you fit, the charity whose hall you did for free. Unglamorous, slow, real, and impossible to penalise you for.

Questions worth asking

Ask these of anyone selling you search work, me included.

How long before I see anything?

Technical fixes can show up in weeks. Ranking for a phrase people actually search usually takes months, and longer in a competitive city. Anyone quoting you a date is guessing.

Can you guarantee page one?

No, and neither can anyone else. Nobody outside Google controls the ranking. A guarantee usually means ranking you for a phrase so specific that nobody searches it, which looks like success in a report and produces no work.

Do I need to write a blog every week?

Almost certainly not. Most small businesses get further from a handful of pages that answer real buying questions properly than from weekly posts nobody reads. Publish when you have something to say.

Is this a monthly thing forever?

The fixing is a project with an end. Keeping up with it — new pages, checking nothing has broken, watching what people actually search — is optional and month to month.

What if I am in a competitive city?

Then broad phrases are expensive and slow, and the winnable work is specific: the exact service, the exact area, the questions your competitors answer badly. That is where a small business can still win.

Do you use AI to write the pages?

As a drafting tool, the way you would use a spellchecker. Nothing goes live that has not been rewritten to sound like you and checked for accuracy, because pages written entirely by a machine read like it and say nothing only you would know.
